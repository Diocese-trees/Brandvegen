# Don't Sound Like AI - Cold Outreach Linter

Run every cold email, hiring-manager pitch, and follow-up draft against this file before sending. Flag every violation, explain why it breaks the rule, and rewrite cleanly.

---

## 1. Banned AI Words

Delete or replace every word below with plain, everyday verbs and concrete nouns:

`delve`, `realm`, `harness`, `tapestry`, `paradigm`, `cutting-edge`, `revolutionize`, `landscape`, `potential`, `findings`, `intricate`, `showcasing`, `crucial`, `pivotal`, `surpass`, `meticulously`, `vibrant`, `unparalleled`, `underscore`, `leverage`, `synergy`, `innovative`, `game-changer`, `testament`, `commendable`, `meticulous`, `highlight`, `emphasize`, `boast`, `groundbreaking`, `align`, `foster`, `showcase`, `enhance`, `holistic`, `garner`, `accentuate`, `pioneering`, `trailblazing`, `unleash`, `versatile`, `transformative`, `redefine`, `seamless`, `optimize`, `scalable`, `robust`, `breakthrough`, `empower`, `streamline`, `intelligent`, `smart`, `next-gen`, `frictionless`, `elevate`, `adaptive`, `effortless`, `data-driven`, `insightful`, `proactive`, `mission-critical`, `visionary`, `disruptive`, `reimagine`, `agile`, `customizable`, `unprecedented`, `intuitive`, `leading-edge`, `synergize`, `democratize`, `accelerate`, `state-of-the-art`, `dynamic`, `cloud-native`, `immersive`, `predictive`, `proprietary`, `plug-and-play`, `turnkey`, `future-proof`, `AI-powered`, `next-generation`, `always-on`, `results-driven`, `unlock`, `grab`, `solution`

### Banned Weakener Words

Cut fillers that dilute direct statements:

`quietly`, `truly`, `simply`, `genuinely`, `really`, `actually`, `basically`, and `just` when used as a softener.

## 2. Banned Phrases

Never open, close, or bump an email with these phrases:

- `quick question`
- `honest question`
- `quick one`
- `hope this email finds you well`
- `dear sir/madam`
- `last chance`
- `only today`
- `act now`
- `just bumping this`
- `circling back`
- `following up`
- `checking in`
- `gentle reminder`
- `haven't heard from you`
- `any thoughts?`
- `did you see my last email`

---

## 3. Banned Sentence Shapes

Rewrite immediately if any draft uses these synthetic structures:

- "Most people do X. The few who win do Y."
- "Stop doing X. Start doing Y."
- "It's not X. It's Y."
- "Here's the truth: [obvious thing]."
- "If you're not doing X, you're already behind."
- "You don't need more X. You need Y."
- "This is the thing that decides everything."
- Two questions stacked inside one email.
- Fake scarcity (e.g., "2 spots left this week") unless a real capacity limit exists.
- Hedged cost statements ("a chance for human error", "can lead to missed opportunities", "risks things slipping through the cracks"). State what actually happens as a fact.

---

## 4. Formatting & Deliverability Rules

Cold emails must be **plain text only**:

- **No** em-dashes or en-dashes.
- **No** bold, italics, bullets, headings, or hashtags.
- **No** exclamation marks.
- **No** links, calendar links, images, logos, attachments, GIFs, or videos before the recipient replies.
- **No** open-tracking pixels.
- **No** word `stop` in the opt-out line (use `nope` instead).
- Write your domain in square brackets inside a plain-text signature (e.g., `Founder, Northgate [northgate.com]`).
- Use contractions (`I've`, `it's`, `you're`), numerals (`5 accounts`, `14 calls`), varied short sentences, and a 5th-grade reading level.

---

## 5. The 8 Pre-Send Audit Questions

Answer every question honestly before finalizing any draft:

1. Is there a banned word, phrase, or sentence shape in it?
2. Is any sentence longer than one breath when read out loud?
3. Is every number one you can stand behind and verify live?
4. Did you promise something you cannot actually deliver?
5. Would the reader know from the first sentence that this was written to them?
6. Does it read like a WhatsApp message to a smart colleague, or like marketing?
7. What is the exact word count, counting the greeting and excluding the P.S. and signature? (Target: 74 to 80 words; max under 90.)
8. **Which rule did I come closest to breaking?** (Answer this straight to catch subtle leaks.)

---

## 6. Pre-Send Checklists

### Before Email 1 Goes
- [ ] One pain point you actually know they have
- [ ] First sentence under 15 words and readable in one breath
- [ ] Second sentence states what the problem costs as a fact (~10 words)
- [ ] Proof line connects directly to the named problem
- [ ] Client named with permission, or cleanly anonymized
- [ ] Country named only when relevant to the recipient
- [ ] Every number is verifiable and binding
- [ ] One easy CTA, no meeting ask, no calendar link
- [ ] Plain text, plain signature, domain in square brackets
- [ ] 74 to 80 words (greeting counted; P.S. and signature excluded)
- [ ] Read out loud with zero stumbles

### Before Every Follow-Up
- [ ] Stands completely on its own without requiring them to scroll up
- [ ] Brings something new (new proof, new angle, or new micro-asset)
- [ ] Uses a different case study if available
- [ ] Asks exactly one question
- [ ] Nothing attached, nothing linked
- [ ] Shorter than the email before it
